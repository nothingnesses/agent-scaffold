### `user-prompts-pointer-and-drift-coverage`: name `audit.md` in the shipped prompt list, distinguish it from the `agent-flow audit` subcommand, and widen the drift guard to the `.agents/user-prompts/` copies

THREE ITEMS, HELD AS ONE STEP RATHER THAN THREE. All three concern the `.agents/user-prompts/` family, all three are small, and all three have a deterministic oracle, so holding them together costs one review loop instead of three (Minimal by default). All three were found during the 0.0.3 release work, all three were deliberately left alone there, and none is fixed by the record you are reading.

`B` is independent of the other two and can be dropped without them. `A` and `C` are not independent of each other: `C`'s guidance half is a clause inside the sentence `A` adds, so `C` costs one clause once `A` is done, and `C` taken alone would have to author `A`'s sentence first, since a list that does not name `audit.md` cannot distinguish it from anything. `A` and `C` together change what `agent-flow scaffold` emits and what `agent-flow --help` prints, so the step owes one CHANGELOG entry, worked out and sited under `C`. `B` owes none.

### `A`: `audit.md` is not named in the shipped getting-started prompt list

`pack/AGENTS.md` opens with a getting-started paragraph that names the human-invoked prompts by filename, and a later paragraph that names the ones carrying state across a break. Between them they name six: `kickoff.md`, `explore.md` and `review.md` in the first, `pause.md`, `compaction-prep.md` and `resume.md` in the second. Measured on the tree: `audit.md` is named zero times in `pack/AGENTS.md`, zero times in the generated root `AGENTS.md`, and zero times in `.agents/AGENTS.reference.md`, while `kickoff.md` is named in all three. So a user whose only entry point is the shipped guidance cannot discover the seventh prompt from it. The README's scaffolded-layout block does list all seven, which is why acceptance criterion 5 of `audit-user-prompt` was met while this gap stayed open.

THIS DOES NOT RE-OPEN THE SELF-CONTAINED-VERSUS-ENTRY-MODE DECISION `audit-user-prompt` settled, and the distinction is the whole reason it is safe to do. That decision was about where the audit DISCIPLINES live, and it put them in the prompt so the shipped guidance would not grow a fifth mode paragraph. What is missing here is a POINTER, one clause naming a file, which carries no discipline into the guidance and adds no mode, no role and no phase.

THE COST, STATED SO IT IS WEIGHED RATHER THAN ASSUMED. `pack/AGENTS.md` is the source of the generated guidance, so editing it widens the regeneration set to the root `AGENTS.md` and `.agents/AGENTS.reference.md`, which the drift guard's checks 1 and 2 pin. The 2026-08-13 audit's recommendation 8 wants the shipped guidance cut rather than grown; one clause against that target is negligible, and the alternative of leaving the prompt undiscoverable from the guidance is the worse trade. Name the trade in the outcome either way.

ACCEPTANCE, each executable:

1. `audit.md` is named by filename in `pack/AGENTS.md`, in the same form the other six use.
2. The root `AGENTS.md` and `.agents/AGENTS.reference.md` are regenerated in the same change, and `cargo test` passes, which is what makes the drift guard's checks 1 and 2 assert the regeneration happened.
3. Nothing else in the guidance moves. No mode paragraph, no role and no phase is added.

### `B`: the `.agents/user-prompts/` copies sit outside the whole-file drift guard

PRE-EXISTING, and documented as a scope call in the module that owns it rather than discovered here. `src/agents_md_drift.rs` runs three comparisons. Checks 1 and 2 pin the committed root `AGENTS.md` and `.agents/AGENTS.reference.md` against a fresh render. Check 3 filters the rendered asset set by `PROMPT_DEST_PREFIX`, which is `.agents/prompts/`, so it covers the role prompts and nothing else. The module's COMPLEMENT rule states the consequence directly and names the `.agents/user-prompts/` copies as one of its illustrations. So nothing pins the committed `.agents/user-prompts/audit.md` against `pack/user-prompts/audit.md`, and the same holds for the other six copies: an edit to a committed copy, or a pack edit without a regeneration, ships a divergence that no gate reports.

THE MODULE HAS ALREADY PRICED THE FIX AND THE PRICE IS WHAT MAKES THIS WORTH DOING NOW. Its own rule records that widening to the Markdown asset copies is a small change to check 3's filter, because they are prose under the same prettier settings and already satisfy the `assert_no_unprotected_construct` precondition, whereas the `.toml` copies under `.agents/` would need a comparison of their own and are NOT in scope here. Widening the filter also inherits the module's accepted `R1` residual, which is that check 3 maps render to committed and asserts nothing in the other direction, so a committed copy the pinned render does not emit stays invisible to it. That residual is not this step's to close.

THE ALTERNATIVE IS TO LEAVE IT, and it is not unreasonable: the gap is documented where a maintainer meets it, and a user-prompt copy going stale ships worse guidance rather than worse behaviour. Take that option only by writing it down as a decision, because the module's rule already anticipates the widening and an undocumented no is indistinguishable from an oversight.

ACCEPTANCE, each executable:

1. Check 3 covers the `.agents/user-prompts/` copies as well as the `.agents/prompts/` ones, by widening the filter rather than by adding a hand-written list, so the check stays self-extending when the pack gains a prompt.
2. Red then green, demonstrated rather than asserted: hand-edit one committed `.agents/user-prompts/` copy, show the guard fails, revert, show it passes. The red state lands as evidence in the outcome.
3. The module doc's COVERAGE block is updated in the same change, since it currently states these copies as an illustration of what is NOT covered, and that sentence becomes false. The `.toml` copies stay out of scope and stay named as out of scope.
4. `cargo test` passes and no other guarded comparison changes.

### `C`: `audit` names two unrelated things, and neither name says which one (`Q-77`)

THE GAP, MEASURED ON THE TREE rather than asserted. `agent-flow audit` is the advisory code-value report (`src/audit.rs`, built by `code-value-audit-static`) and `.agents/user-prompts/audit.md` is the pasted prompt carrying the pre-registered measurement method (built by `audit-user-prompt`). They share no machinery, no input and no output. What each place says today: the subcommand's help text is the single-paragraph doc comment at `src/main.rs:424`, and it names the prompt zero times; `pack/AGENTS.md` contains the string `audit` zero times in any case, so the shipped guidance names neither of the two; the generated root `AGENTS.md` and `.agents/AGENTS.reference.md` contain it once each, at line 145, as the word `auditable` inside the decision-receipt schema, which is a third and unrelated sense. `README.md` is the only file in the tree that names both, at line 49 in the scaffolded-layout block and at lines 216 and 270 to 284 in the "Auditing code value" section, 221 lines apart with no cross-reference between them. So a user who reads `agent-flow --help` and then the shipped guidance has the word and nothing else, which is the gap `Q-77` records.

THE DECISION THIS IMPLEMENTS. `Q-77` (human, 2026-08-15) chose to keep both names and to distinguish them in the `audit` subcommand's help text and in the shipped prompt list, over renaming the subcommand, over renaming the prompt, and over ruling the collision acceptable in the queue alone. The queue entry carries the reasoning, what was rejected and why, and the principle that argues the other way. This item is that decision's whole implementation, and it adds nothing the decision did not choose.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT RE-OPEN. It renames nothing, in either direction, and it does not settle whether the two names should differ; the collision stands and `Q-77` says so in as many words. It adds no mode, no role and no phase to the shipped guidance, which is the same boundary item `A` holds and for the same reason. It does not touch `pack/user-prompts/audit.md`: that prompt's opening paragraph already states what it is for, and a reader who is already inside the prompt is not choosing between it and a subcommand.

THE COST, STATED SO IT IS WEIGHED RATHER THAN ASSUMED. The guidance half is a clause inside a sentence item `A` is adding, so against `A` its marginal cost is one clause and no extra regeneration. The help half is one sentence added to a doc comment that already runs to three, in a subcommand list entry that is already the longest of the seven, so `agent-flow --help` gets longer for every user including the ones who will never paste a prompt. What that buys is priced in `Q-77`: the alternative is the user who cannot tell the two apart at all.

TWO SITES, SPECIFIED HERE SO THE IMPLEMENTER AUTHORS PROSE RATHER THAN DECIDING SCOPE.

SITE 1 IS THE HELP TEXT AT `src/main.rs:424`, the doc comment on the `Audit(AuditArgs)` variant of `enum Command`. Append one sentence to the EXISTING paragraph rather than starting a new one. Clap derives a subcommand's short `about` from the first paragraph of its doc comment and the `long_about` from the whole, so a second paragraph would reach `agent-flow audit --help` and never `agent-flow --help`, and `agent-flow --help` is the path `Q-77` names. The sentence states that this subcommand is unrelated to the `audit.md` user prompt, names `.agents/user-prompts/audit.md` in full so it is greppable and pasteable, and says in one clause what that prompt does. NOTHING PINS THE HELP STRINGS: no test under `tests/` reads any help output, and the only other `about` string in the tree is the top-level one at `src/main.rs:401`. So `cargo test` neither catches a mistake here nor objects to a correct change, which is why criterion 1 runs the built binary instead.

SITE 2 IS THE PROMPT LIST IN `pack/AGENTS.md`, inside the getting-started paragraph at line 7 that item `A` is already editing. The distinguishing text is a subordinate clause on `A`'s pointer sentence, naming `agent-flow audit` and what it reports. It is not its own sentence and not its own paragraph, which is the boundary `A`'s acceptance criterion 3 sets. `pack/AGENTS.md` already names the binary once in prose, `agent-flow render` at line 30, so naming a second subcommand there introduces no new kind of reference into the shipped guidance.
