# Audit, 2026-08-13: when the loop turned

Rendered view: <https://claude.ai/code/artifact/5343821a-bedf-452b-be53-8bf0a495bcea>. Companion methods reference: `2026-08-13-audit-measurement-methods.md` in this directory.

Human-invoked audit of the dogfooded workflow, run at `d3b4ca8`. This is `workflow-calibration` evidence and it supersedes nothing in `calibration-analysis.md` (2026-07-31), which it re-tests with 12 more days of data and corrects in one place.

## Method

Nine agents, each isolated in its own detached git worktree, read-only against the repository. Each was told to treat `agent-scaffold.ledger.md` as hypotheses rather than facts, because it is largely the orchestrator's account of its own performance, and to prefer git history where the two disagree. Every load-bearing claim was re-verified by the orchestrator against primary sources before it entered this record.

The criteria and the failure condition were fixed and published BEFORE any measurement, so they could not be chosen to fit the result. That pre-registration is the single control that makes this record worth anything.

Auditor questions: capability versus process (A1), finding taxonomy (A2), convergence realisation (A3), flow and velocity (A4), reviewer productivity and residual estimation (A5), defect escapes and value (A6), the product judged independently (A7), cost accounting (A8), plus a ledger comb spawned by A3.

## The pre-registered failure condition

Recorded before measurement. The workflow is degenerate if ALL THREE hold together, because each alone has an innocent explanation:

1. The designed convergence rule almost never fires, so loops close by human decree rather than by measurement.
2. The self-referential finding rate does not fall across rounds within a loop.
3. The share of findings that are defects in shipped behaviour is near zero.

## Verdict

THE WORKFLOW WAS NOT DEGENERATE. IT BECAME DEGENERATE, AND THE TRANSITION IS DATABLE TO ROUGHLY 2026-07-19 THROUGH 07-31.

All three conditions hold for the project's final two weeks. None holds for the project as a whole. Five auditors working on different questions, without coordinating, independently identified the same fortnight.

| Measure | Before | After | Turn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Documentation to code, by bytes | 1.28 : 1 | 5.17 : 1 | 07-19 |
| Net Rust delivered per week | +3,733 | +48 | 07-19 |
| Process to product churn ratio | 1.95 | 37.46 | 07-23 |
| Risky loops converging | 17 of 24 | 0 of 8 | 07-31 |
| Valid findings per round | 1.58 | 7.58 | 07-30 |
| Steps generated by the process itself | 8.3% | 54.2% | monotonic |

The last two rows are the mechanism. A converging loop finds fewer things each round. This one began finding MORE, because half of what each round found was text the previous round's fix had just written, and because more than half of all new work was by then generated by the loop itself.

Eleven consecutive days, 2026-08-01 to 08-11, produced zero completed steps across 196 commits.

## Condition 1: convergence

NOT MET project-wide. MET decisively for the recent era.

Of 100 review loops, 76 converged by reaching their required streak: 86.4% of `low_risk` and 55.9% of `risky`. Risky loops opened on or before 2026-07-31: 24 loops, 17 converged. Opened after: 8 loops, 0 converged. All six increments of `workflow-enforcement-tier` closed by waiver.

The reason is the cap, not the bar. Transition rates measured on the live log:

```
P(round 1 clean)                  4/34  = 11.8%
P(clean | previous had findings)  25/64 = 39.1%
P(clean | previous was clean)     19/23 = 82.6%
```

The second clean round is the EASY one. After a clean round the artifact stops being touched (21 of those 23 rounds carry `changed_since_prev: false`), so the next round is a pure re-sample. The binding constraint is the first clean round at 39%, against a cap allowing four attempts at it. Six risky loops ended their final round clean at streak 1 and were waived anyway; at the observed 83% extension rate most would have converged with one more round.

Reproduce with `docs/plans/workflow-calibration.explorations/` scripts, or directly: group `type:"round"` records by structured `increment` id else `task`, preserve log order, and count transitions among `risky` rounds.

CORRECTION TO `calibration-analysis.md`. That analysis recommended keeping the cap at 5, resting on two observations. One is a grouping artifact: it merged three `plan_review` rounds carrying no increment id with three `work_review` rounds carrying `increment = "structured-skeleton-inc6"`, both under `task = "structured-skeleton"`, into one spurious six-round series. Those are two artifacts, one of which converged at round 2. That leaves `prompt-drift-guard-inc1` as the sole observation behind "do not raise the cap", and it reached streak 1 at round 6.

The BAR is now settled at 2 by the analysis's own pre-registered rule ("any single medium-or-worse finding in an enforced second round settles it at 2 permanently"). There are now 23 enforced second rounds and one returned a `medium`: a test named for the branch it claimed to cover, asserting a red-then-green in its own doc comment, printing `ok`, and demonstrating nothing.

## Condition 2: self-reference

MET. 49% strict, 61% broad, across 189 of 211 round-2-onward findings.

Half of every finding after round 1 is raised against text the previous fix pass in the same loop wrote. The rate does not fall reliably because it is controlled by WHAT THE FIX PASS DOES, not by how many rounds have run:

- Deletion-only and number-edit-only fixes re-seed at close to zero. One loop authored zero new sentences and zero new bullets in its round 4 fix and returned zero findings in the round after.
- Fix passes that author prose re-seed at close to 100%. One loop measured 8 of 8 after round 1.
- One acceptance check produced a finding after all five times it was edited, unbroken.

This is the single highest-leverage variable found in the audit, and the project had already measured it five separate times without acting on it.

## Condition 3: behaviour defects

MET for the recent era, not project-wide. 12 to 16% overall; 5% in the final era.

Two thirds of all valid findings are claim defects: a comment, doc, changelog line, error string or plan sentence saying something false about correct code. Six consecutive late loops, 154 findings across 22 rounds, found ZERO defects in what the software does, by their own triagers' explicit statements. Thirty-one percent of all findings come from reviewing PLANNING DOCUMENTS, which supply 20 of the project's 32 high-severity findings.

## What the loop is worth

Roughly 27 real defects that would otherwise have shipped, about 4% of its 624 valid findings, concentrated in the loops that implemented new logic.

The strongest single item: a wrong grouping key in the W3 convergence check, which made the tool reject data its own design defines as convergent, producing 26 problems on the project's own valid log against 3 after the fix. The pre-fix tree passed 108 tests with clean clippy. No mechanical check the repository owned could see it. A reviewer found it.

Also caught: four distinct write-escapes outside the named output directory, two of them found in rounds 2 and 3 rather than round 1; three cross-project data leaks at exit 0, one of which printed this repository's own resume state into a foreign project's agent brief; and a circular exemption boundary in W4 where a forgotten receipt would move its own exemption.

Independent evidence the loop produced quality: `cargo-mutants` scores 144 of 150 viable mutants caught, 96.0%, on `src/workflow.rs` and `src/plan/source.rs`.

Against that: three of the worst false-green defects in the project were found by DOGFOODING and an architecture audit, not by review rounds. Any engineer running the tool against a second project on day one finds them faster.

## What the loop cannot do, measured

`validate --workflow` verifies the round log's internal arithmetic. It does not verify that any review occurred, and cannot.

Reproduced on a copy of this repository's own plan and log. Flipping `validation-constraints` to `complete` refuses correctly. Appending TWO HAND-WRITTEN JSON LINES claiming clean rounds, with no review, no findings file and no commit reference, returns `workflow invariants hold` at exit 0. Appending a third line declaring a round with a `critical` severity, zero valid findings, outcome `clean` and no `dismissal_recheck` record also passes at exit 0.

The mechanism: `dismissal_recheck` appears 4 times in `src/metrics.rs` and 0 times in `src/workflow.rs`. The record type parses and no check ever reads it.

Compounding this, six rounds are recorded `outcome: "clean"` while carrying `valid_findings > 0`, and every one is at `consecutive_clean: 1`, so each advanced a streak. `validate` recomputes the streak from `outcome` alone and never cross-checks it against `valid_findings`.

## Cost

- Review prose exceeds production source 7.4 to 1 by bytes. 461 files, 68,752 lines, all subsequently deleted.
- 74.3% of 1,022 commits touched neither product code nor tests. The product-touching share fell from 50% in week one to 9.8% in week four.
- 48 of 77 work units delivered zero or negative net source, consuming 48% of all review prose ever written.
- `workflow-enforcement-tier-inc4`: 20 review agents, 7,295 lines of review prose, 53 commits, 5 days, net -4 source lines.
- The ledger is 858 KB growing at 27 KB per day. 29% is explicitly marked not-live, and 27% of its paragraphs exist to overturn an earlier paragraph.
- Over half the metrics log's field values are validated and never read. Three of seven record types have never been written.
- 83 human decision points, accelerating to 4 per day, one per 32 net lines of source in the endgame. Nothing measures this.

Flow efficiency is 15 to 28%, most likely 24%, which is ABOVE the commonly published knowledge-work norm. Waiting on the human is not the problem. The 78x fall in weekly output decomposes as 2.25x less time worked and 34.5x lower output per hour worked.

## The fact that frames the rest

One release, `v0.0.1`, on project day 2, 937 commits ago. `Cargo.toml` is still 0.0.1. Only 194 of those 937 commits touched `src/` or `pack/`. Ten commits touched `pack/` in the last twenty days.

The strongest defence of the last month is that a workflow tool's workflow content IS its product, so guidance written into `pack/` is capability rather than overhead. That argument is sound in principle and fails on one point of fact: shipped content is only shipped when someone can install it.

## Claims this audit falsified

Six of the orchestrator's own, recorded because an audit that only finds other people's errors is not an audit.

| Claimed | Measured |
| --- | --- |
| Convergence almost never fires | 76% of loops converged; the failure is recent, not general |
| Flow efficiency will be low, waiting dominates | 24%, above the norm; waiting is not the problem |
| Fix passes re-seed, so two clean rounds is unreachable | P(clean after clean) = 82.6%; the second is the easy one |
| The data for capture-recapture has been collected | No capture history exists; the estimator cannot be applied |
| Reviewer yield falls sharply past two or three | Flat from N=2 to N=3; the classical curve does not reproduce |
| Declaring the fold increments unblocked the status flip | Mechanically inert; identical output with them deleted |

The last happened DURING the audit. On 2026-08-12 the orchestrator declared two `[[step.increment]]` entries, measured a refusal by probe, and recorded the refusal as the declaration's effect. Deleting both declarations, or changing their declared `risk_class` from `risky` to `low_risk`, produces byte-identical output. The refusal came from the round log throughout. `Increment.risk_class` is documented at `src/plan/source.rs:271` as setting the required streak and has no readers.

## Two standing rules that have never been exercised

- Harness diversity: 277 reviewer passes recorded `claude-code`, 30 recorded nothing, and ZERO recorded any other harness. The field has held one value for its entire life.
- Lens identity: 112 distinct `role` labels across 307 passes, of which 80 ran exactly once. Which lens finds what is unmeasurable as recorded.

Against those, the separate-triager rule earns its cost plainly: the triager altered 28.9% of all dispositions, dismissing 49, lowering 53 severities, raising 16, originating 6, and ruling 41 valid-but-out-of-scope. It overturns downward 3.3 to 1.

## Recommendations

Ordered by value. The first is not a workflow change.

1. Ship `v0.0.2`. Every argument that the workflow content is the product depends on someone being able to install it.
2. Stop reviewing plan documents with the code loop. Near-zero behaviour defects, near-100% re-seeding, 20 of 32 highs.
3. Constrain what a fix pass may do. This one variable drives re-seeding from 0% to 100%.
4. Raise the cap to 7 and keep the bar at 2.
5. Enforce the dismissal re-check or delete the record type.
6. State what `validate --workflow` actually checks, or make it check something real.
7. Read `[[step.increment]]` or delete it.
8. Cut the shipped guidance to about 2,500 words from 7,734, or 10,370 with instrumentation.
9. Build the three instrumentation fixes scheduled on 2026-07-31 and never built.
10. Measure human decision load and cost per unit delivered.

## Limits

No true escape rate exists, because nothing has shipped and there is no field defect data. Agent time and token cost are recorded nowhere. Whether any third-party user exists is unknown. A residual defect estimate is not available, because the reviewer data lacks capture histories and reviewers are assigned deliberately non-overlapping lenses, which breaks the estimator's independence and homogeneity assumptions together and inflates every result. Setting the population to the observed count, which maximises expected overlap, predicts 83 shared findings against 33 observed.

Finding classification in the first three eras is coarser than the fourth and carries perhaps ten percent error. The fourth era, 46% of the census, was classified finding by finding from complete reads.

This is one project, one maintainer, 34 days. Every rate here is an observation about this codebase, not a law about agent workflows.
